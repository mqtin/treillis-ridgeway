# Reading path

The lattice can be read in every direction. For a first reading, here is a thread of sixteen steps, from the book's question to its conclusion. Each step points to an element; from there, "Path to" shows what it rests on, "Consequences" what follows from it.

## I. What status is

**1.** D01, T001. Status is a ranking of esteem. Status hierarchies are a cultural invention for managing a tension in the human condition: we must cooperate, and we contend over the terms of cooperation.

**2.** T002. Status is relational, social, consensual and public; above all, it is given by others, not taken.

**3.** T003, T004. It organises cooperation, but only "boundedly"; it differs from power and wealth, while linked to them.

## II. Where the schema comes from

**4.** T005, T006. Neither dominance nor prestige explains status hierarchies.

**5.** T007, T008, T009. With three or more, bystanders matter: each has an interest in others deferring to competence, and third parties side against the dominator. A norm is revealed.

**6.** T011. The schema is twofold: a basic norm (the grammar) and status beliefs (the vocabulary).

**7.** T012. The consensus governing the hierarchy bears on what others expect, not on what each thinks they deserve.

## III. Why we care

**8.** T016, T019. The desire for status is a fundamental motive, and insatiable, because status is never secured.

**9.** T020. The schema rewards even low status: whoever defers is respected as reasonable.

## IV. Status beliefs

**10.** T024, T025. Status beliefs bear on what "most people" think; that is what gives them their force.

**11.** T028. They arise from encounters in which one group is systematically advantaged.

**12.** T032. They create a status advantage that passes for merit.

## V. In interaction

**13.** T034, T036. Performance expectations are self-fulfilling, and diffuse characteristics (gender, race, class) frame them from the start.

**14.** T044. Three biases (status, legitimacy, associational preference) accumulate over a lifetime.

## VI. Durable inequality

**15.** T046, T047, T053. Status beliefs consolidate material inequality with difference, give it the power to reproduce itself and legitimate it by merit.

**16.** T059, T060. Status is a double-edged sword; it will not disappear, but group inequality is not inevitable.
